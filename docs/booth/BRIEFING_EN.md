# Rai Mai Phao (Zero-Burn Farm) — Team Briefing

*Everything the team should know before talking to visitors and judges. All prices, yields, costs and PM2.5 figures are **prototype estimates** to be tested on a real machine.*

---

## 1. The project in one breath
Farmers burn crop residue because it is the **cheapest option**. We make residue **worth money**: our 3-in-1 machine **KhumKha** ("worth it" in Thai) in a **village station** turns it into **animal feed, fuel briquettes or compost material**. Farmers sell through **LINE**, and a **local driver** collects it from the farm, like Grab. **Farmers earn, drivers get work, buyers get cheap raw material, everyone gets cleaner air.**

## 3. How it works (5 steps)
1. **Book in LINE**: the farmer reports residue type and weight, and chooses to bring it or book a pickup.
2. **Village pickup**: a registered local driver accepts the job and collects it from the farm.
3. **Weigh, photo, moisture check** at the station: the farmer's QR code is scanned at the machine.
4. **Process** in mode A, B or C.
5. **Farmer chooses**: sell to us, consign, or take it home. A receipt arrives in LINE automatically.

**Station (8 parts):** truck scale · covered drying yard · machine shed · solar roof · lot storage · shop & office · fire safety · parking. Start with **one machine per village**, add more as supply grows.

## 4. How the machine works (top to bottom)
| Height | Part | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 140–180 cm | hopper with safety lid, hand guard, magnet, moisture sensor, touch screen, QR scanner, camera | receive, check, identify the lot |
| 100–140 cm | hammer mill shredder + 2 screens | shred |
| 85–100 cm | motorised diverter | send to A / B / C |
| 30–85 cm | A flat-die pellet press · C straight chute · B heated screw press + cutter | process |
| 0–30 cm | 3 output trays with scales | weigh output, compute yield |
| Back | dust fan + bag, control box, vents | dust, heat, safety |

**Known limits:** one mode at a time (solved with a queue) · switching from fuel to feed needs a flush run · if the shredder fails the whole machine stops (spare blades + automatic technician alert).

## 8. Question bank

### Problem & idea
- **Why do farmers burn?** It's the cheapest and fastest way to clear the field before the next crop. Ploughing it in costs money and earns nothing.
- **Why not just ban burning?** Bans exist, but without a cheaper alternative farmers still burn. We give them a reason not to: income.
- **Who is your customer?** Two sides: farmers who sell residue, and buyers of the products (mushroom farms, dairy co-ops, animal farms, factories with boilers).

### Machine
- **What's the machine called?** KhumKha, Thai for "worth it": it makes not burning worth more than burning. The brand is Rai Mai Phao / Zero-Burn Farm.
- **What does the machine make?** A: animal feed or fuel pellets, B: fuel briquettes, C: chopped material for compost, mushrooms or mulch.
- **How fast is it?** About 15 min per 100 kg for chopping, ~60 min for pellets, ~75 min for briquettes.
- **What if the residue is wet?** Above 20% moisture it's rejected. The station has a drying yard.
- **What if it breaks?** Spare blades, and the system alerts a technician every 200 working hours.
- **Is it safe?** Lid cut-off switch, hand guard, magnet for metal, emergency stop, dust extraction.
- **Is it built?** It's a detailed 3D design and a working software prototype. Building a pilot machine is the next step.

### Business
- **How do you make money?** Margin on products, processing fees, and 10% of pickup fees.
- **How much does it cost?** About 200,000 THB per machine (150k–280k). The station depends on local land and budget.
- **When does it pay back?** Roughly 17 months at 400 kg/day and ~1.2 THB/kg margin. This is an illustration to be tested.
- **Why 1 baht per kg?** It beats burning (zero income) and leaves room for margin after processing.
- **Who pays for the pickup fund?** It comes from product margins, because pickup is part of our raw-material cost. It only applies when farmers sell to us.
- **Why 30%?** So the pickup never eats most of a small farmer's income.
- **What stops people gaming pickups?** Pickup needs at least 300 kg or a full load, and we suggest sharing trips with nearby farms.
- **Competitors?** Big biomass power plants buy residue but only near the plant and in bulk. We go to the village and to small farmers.

### Impact & SDGs
- **Which SDGs?** 3, 8, 12, 13 (also 7 and 15).
- **How do you measure impact?** Every run is logged: kg not burned, PM2.5 avoided (~8 g/kg), farmer income, driver income. It's shown on the dashboard.
- **Is the 8 g/kg PM2.5 figure real?** It's an estimate; we'll replace it with a referenced research value.

### Tech & law
- **Why LINE?** Farmers already use it every day. No new app to install.
- **What about personal data (PDPA)?** Separate consent page, only needed data, masked names in public, and farmers can withdraw consent and delete their data.
- **Can you sell the animal feed?** At first we use it in community farms. Selling it needs registration under Thai feed law.
- **Can the brand be protected?** Yes, "Rai Mai Phao" can be registered as a trademark. The code and 3D model are copyrighted automatically.

### Hard ones
- **Biggest risk?** The numbers are untested. A pilot machine is our next step.
- **What would you do with funding?** Build one pilot machine in a Chiang Rai village, test yields and costs, then fix the numbers.
- **What did you learn?** The real problem isn't awareness, it's economics. Make the right choice the cheaper choice.
